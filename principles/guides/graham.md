# Benjamin Graham: how he decides, and how he looks ahead

Distilled in our own words from *The Intelligent Investor* (revised edition with Jason Zweig's commentary), mainly chapters 1, 8, 11, 12, 14, 15 and 20. Thresholds below are Graham's own (1972); the book's commentary shows how to scale them to today's interest rates.

## The core idea
An investment is a purchase that, after thorough analysis, promises safety of the money put in and a satisfactory return. Anything else is speculation. The central concept is the **margin of safety**: pay enough less than the business is worth that you do not need an accurate forecast, and bad luck or a wrong estimate will not ruin you. Treat the market as a moody partner (Mr. Market) who quotes a price every day: use his offers when they suit you, never take his mood as information about value.

## Before he invests: the seven tests for a cautious investor (ch. 14)

| # | Test | Graham's threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adequate size | Big enough to survive trouble (he used $100 million of annual sales; scale up for today) |
| 2 | Strong financial condition | Current assets at least twice current liabilities, and long-term debt no larger than working capital |
| 3 | Earnings stability | Some profit in each of the past ten years |
| 4 | Dividend record | Uninterrupted payments for at least 20 years |
| 5 | Earnings growth | Per-share profit up at least a third over ten years (three-year averages at each end) |
| 6 | Moderate price to earnings | No more than 15 times the average profit of the last three years |
| 7 | Moderate price to assets | No more than 1.5 times book value, or: P/E times price-to-book no more than 22.5 |

Also: the earnings yield (profit per dollar of price) should at least match the current yield on high-grade bonds, so the stock offers a margin over bonds. The tests deliberately exclude both small, weak companies and popular, expensive ones.

## The more adventurous route (ch. 15)
Bargains: companies priced at two-thirds or less of "net current asset value" (current assets minus all liabilities), bought as a diversified basket of many. Also solid but unloved "secondary" companies with a decent record and no public charm. Both work through a wide gap between price and value, and diversification, because any single one can fail.

## What he avoids
Paying today for growth that has not yet arrived (all estimates that differ from the past should lean low); fair-weather purchases of weak companies at times of high earnings (the chief losses come from buying low quality in good times); relying on one year's earnings or on "extraordinary, non-recurring" items that recur; popular stocks at prices the facts do not support; and acting on forecasts of the market.

## How he looks ahead
- **He does not forecast:** the margin of safety exists to make an accurate forecast unnecessary. He asks how far earnings could fall before the investment stops working.
- **He uses averages:** seven to ten years of earnings smooth out the business cycle and one-off items, so he judges "earning power", not last year.
- **He compares with bonds:** the stock's earnings yield against the safe alternative is the yardstick for whether the future is already priced in.
- **He diversifies when the margin is thin:** a margin only guarantees a better chance of profit than loss, not that any single stock works. More independent positions make the average result more dependable.
- **What would change his mind:** a rising price that eats the margin, deteriorating finance (current ratio, debt), or earnings that were not what they seemed.

## Guidance when reading a stock through his eyes
- **Strong signals:** a record of profits, a solid balance sheet, a price well below a cautious estimate of value, and an earnings yield comfortably above bond yields.
- **Warning signs:** a price that assumes growth far above the record, thin liquidity (current ratio under 2), a price over 15 to 20 times average earnings, a dividend that was cut, and profits that depend on adjustments.
- **The one-line test:** "If my estimate is wrong by a third, do I still do fine?"
